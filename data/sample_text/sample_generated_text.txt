Questions 1-2

A city transportation survey found that commuters who take the train to work report lower stress levels than commuters who drive. However, the survey did not control for commute distance, and commuters with shorter trips generally report less stress regardless of transportation mode.

1. Which one of the following, if true, most weakens the argument that taking the train reduces commuter stress?

(A) Train commuters are more likely than drivers to have shorter commute distances.
(B) Many commuters choose driving because they need flexibility during the workday.
(C) Employers in the city increasingly subsidize public transit passes.
(D) Some people cannot afford to commute by train.
(E) Many workplaces do not reimburse employees for train fares.

2. The argument depends on assuming which one of the following?

(A) Commuters accurately report their own stress levels.
(B) Train service in the city is generally reliable.
(C) Differences in commute distance could affect reported stress levels.
(D) Driving is more expensive than taking the train.
(E) Employers prefer workers who use public transportation.
